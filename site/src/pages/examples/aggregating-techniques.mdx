# Aggregating Techniques

<SchemaReference db="pnw_database.sqlite" />

## Numerical summaries

Aggregate functions (AVG, SUM, MIN, MAX, COUNT) collapse multiple rows into summary statistics.

### Aggregating 1: What's the average town size in each state?

*Scenario: Compare typical town sizes between Oregon and Washington.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example27"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT state,
       ROUND(AVG(population_2020_census), 0) AS avg_population
  FROM pnw_towns
 GROUP BY state;`}
/>

### Aggregating 2: What's the total urban population by state?

*Scenario: How much of each state's population lives in incorporated towns?*

<SqlExercise
  id="example28"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT state,
       SUM(population_2020_census) AS total_town_population
  FROM pnw_towns
 GROUP BY state;`}
/>

### Aggregating 3: Find the smallest incorporated town

*Scenario: A journalist wants to profile the smallest town in the Pacific Northwest.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example29"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT town, state, population_2020_census
  FROM pnw_towns
 WHERE population_2020_census = (SELECT MIN(population_2020_census) FROM pnw_towns);`}
/>

### Aggregating 4: Find the largest city

*Scenario: Identify the region's largest metropolitan center.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example30"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT town, state, population_2020_census
  FROM pnw_towns
 WHERE population_2020_census = (SELECT MAX(population_2020_census) FROM pnw_towns);`}
/>

### Aggregating 5: Summary statistics for county land areas

*Scenario: Get a complete statistical overview of county sizes.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example31"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS num_counties,
       MIN(land_area_sq_mi) AS smallest_county,
       MAX(land_area_sq_mi) AS largest_county,
       ROUND(AVG(land_area_sq_mi), 0) AS avg_size,
       SUM(land_area_sq_mi) AS total_area
  FROM pnw_counties;`}
/>

## Categorical summaries

MIN and MAX work on text too, returning alphabetically first/last values.

### Aggregating 6: Which county was established first and last?

*Scenario: Historical research on regional development.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example32"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT MIN(year_established) AS earliest_county,
       MAX(year_established) AS newest_county
  FROM pnw_counties;`}
/>

### Aggregating 7: Find the actual earliest and newest counties

*Scenario: Get the county names, not just the years.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example33"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT county, state, year_established
  FROM pnw_counties
 WHERE year_established = (SELECT MIN(year_established) FROM pnw_counties)
    OR year_established = (SELECT MAX(year_established) FROM pnw_counties)
 ORDER BY year_established;`}
  requireOrdering
/>

## Rounding summaries

ROUND helps create cleaner, more readable output for reports and presentations.

### Aggregating 8: Calculate and round population density

*Scenario: Create a readable density report (people per square mile).*

<SqlExercise
  id="example34a"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT town, 
       state,
       population_2020_census,
       land_area_sq_mi,
       ROUND(population_2020_census / land_area_sq_mi, 1) AS people_per_sq_mile
  FROM pnw_towns
 WHERE land_area_sq_mi > 0
 ORDER BY people_per_sq_mile DESC;`}
  requireOrdering
/>

### Aggregating 9: Round to whole numbers for simpler reporting

*Scenario: Executive summaries often need whole numbers.*

<SqlExercise
  id="example34b"
  db="pnw_database.sqlite"
  solution={`SELECT state,
       ROUND(AVG(population_2020_census), 0) AS avg_town_pop,
       ROUND(AVG(land_area_sq_mi), 0) AS avg_town_area
  FROM pnw_towns
 GROUP BY state;`}
/>
